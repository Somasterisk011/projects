Business Formation & Compliance

Starting or running a business involves more than registration. Many legal problems arise at the formation and compliance stage. Early legal advice helps avoid future disputes, penalties, and losses.

Common Issues Clients Face

  • Confusion about choosing the right business structure
  • Starting a business without proper registration
  • Receiving ROC / GST / government notices
  • Disputes between partners or directors
  • Missing mandatory filings and compliances
  • Banking or funding issues due to improper documentation

How an Advocate Helps

  • Choosing the right legal structure (Company, LLP, Partnership, Proprietorship)
  • Handles registration and incorporation
  • Drafts clear agreements between founders or partners
  • Ensures statutory compliance from day one
  • Helps avoid future disputes and penalties

Primary Steps Clients Should Follow

Step 1: Choose the Right Business Structure

The structure is selected based on the nature of business, number of partners, investment plans, and liability concerns.

  • Private Limited Company
  • Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
  • Partnership Firm
  • Proprietorship
Step 2: Register the Business Properly
  • Name approval
  • Government registration
  • Statutory numbers such as PAN and GST
Step 3: Draft Essential Legal Documents

Written agreements prevent disputes and clearly define roles and responsibilities.

  • Partnership or Shareholder Agreements
  • Founders’ Agreements
  • MOA & AOA (for companies)
Step 4: Ensure Ongoing Compliance
  • Annual filings
  • Tax returns
  • Board resolutions
  • Statutory disclosures

Mistakes Clients Should Avoid

  • Starting a business without legal registration
  • Choosing a structure based on informal advice
  • No written agreement between partners
  • Ignoring government notices
  • Mixing personal and business finances
  • Delaying compliance filings

Documents Required

Personal Documents
  • Aadhaar Card
  • PAN Card
  • Address proof (utility bill / bank statement)
    • (* Documents available with the individual)
Business Documents
  • Business address proof
  • Rent agreement or ownership proof
  • NOC from property owner (if rented)
    • (* Documents from landlord/premise owner or property records)
Partner / Director Documents
  • Identity and address proof of all partners/directors
  • Passport-sized photographs
    • (* From partners/directors)
Registration Documents
  • Incorporation forms
  • Agreements
  • Declarations and affidavits
    • (* Prepared and filed by advocate with authorities)

Where These Documents Are Filed

  • Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA)
  • GST Department
  • Income Tax Department
  • Other relevant authorities

Why Early Legal Consultation Matters

  • Prevents future disputes
  • Saves time and money
  • Protects personal assets
  • Builds trust with investors and banks
  • Keeps the business legally secure

Contracts & Agreements

Contracts and agreements govern every business relationship. Most disputes arise due to unclear terms, verbal commitments, or poorly drafted agreements. Proper legal drafting and review help protect business interests and avoid costly conflicts.

Common Issues Clients Face

  • Agreements drafted without legal review
  • Reliance on verbal promises
  • Unclear payment, scope, or timeline clauses
  • Breach of contract by vendors, clients, or partners
  • Difficulties in enforcing agreement terms
  • Disputes arising during termination of contracts

How an Advocate Helps

  • Drafts clear and enforceable agreements
  • Reviews existing contracts to identify legal risks
  • Advises on rights, obligations, and liabilities
  • Issues legal notices for breach of contract
  • Assists in negotiation, arbitration, or litigation

Primary Steps Clients Should Follow

Step 1: Identify the Purpose of the Agreement

Clearly define the business relationship, scope of work, and expectations before entering into any contract.

Step 2: Draft the Agreement Properly
  • Roles and responsibilities
  • Payment terms and timelines
  • Confidentiality and non-disclosure clauses
  • Dispute resolution mechanism
Step 3: Review Before Signing

An advocate reviews clauses that may create future liabilities or expose the business to legal risk.

Step 4: Execute the Agreement Correctly
  • Proper signing by authorized persons
  • Stamping as per applicable law
  • Maintaining executed copies for records

Mistakes Clients Should Avoid

  • Relying only on verbal agreements
  • Using copied or generic templates
  • Ignoring termination and exit clauses
  • Signing agreements without legal review
  • Delaying action after breach of contract

Common Business Agreements

  • Service Agreements
  • Vendor and Supplier Agreements
  • Partnership and Shareholder Agreements
  • Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDA)
  • Joint Venture Agreements
  • Consultancy and Employment Contracts
    • (* Used where required to clearly define roles, payments, and responsibilities.)

Documents Required

Owner & Business Details
  • Business registration details
  • Identity and address proof of parties
  • Authorized signatory details
    • (* Documents provided by the Business owner)
Transaction Details
  • Scope of work or service description
  • Commercial terms and payment structure
  • Duration and renewal terms
    • (* Shared by both contracting parties)
Drafting & Execution Documents
  • Draft agreements
  • Final executed copies
  • Stamp duty documents
    • (* Prepared and finalized with advocate assistance)

Why Legal Review of Contracts Matters

  • Prevents future disputes
  • Ensures enforceability of terms
  • Protects business rights and assets
  • Reduces legal and financial risks

Corporate Disputes & Fraud

Corporate disputes and fraud issues arise when trust breaks down within a business or between business parties. These matters often involve financial loss, misuse of authority, or breach of legal duties. Early legal intervention helps prevent escalation and protects business interests.

Common Issues Clients Face

  • Cheating or misconduct by business partners or directors
  • Misuse or diversion of company funds
  • Breach of shareholder or partnership agreements
  • Oppression or exclusion of minority shareholders
  • Forgery, misrepresentation, or false assurances
  • Criminal complaints arising from business transactions

How an Advocate Helps

  • Examines agreements, financial records, and transactions
  • Advises on civil and criminal remedies
  • Issues legal notices and represents before authorities
  • Handles shareholder and partnership disputes
  • Assists in settlement, mediation, or litigation

Primary Steps Clients Should Follow

Step 1: Identify the Nature of the Dispute or Fraud

Determine whether the issue is contractual, financial, managerial, or involves criminal elements.

Step 2: Preserve Evidence
  • Agreements and incorporation documents
  • Financial statements and bank records
  • Emails, messages, and transaction proofs
Step 3: Seek Legal Advice Before Taking Action

Premature or emotional actions may weaken your legal position. An advocate advises the correct legal route.

Step 4: Initiate Legal Proceedings or Settlement
  • Legal notice or reply
  • Civil suit or criminal complaint
  • Arbitration or negotiated settlement

Mistakes Clients Should Avoid

  • Ignoring early warning signs of misconduct
  • Relying only on verbal assurances
  • Delaying legal action despite clear losses
  • Destroying or altering evidence
  • Taking steps without legal consultation

Common Corporate Disputes & Fraud Matters

  • Shareholder and partnership disputes
  • Oppression and mismanagement cases
  • Business cheating and fraud
  • Breach of fiduciary duties by directors
  • Financial misrepresentation and forgery

M&A and Business Transfers

Mergers, acquisitions, and business transfers involve the legal process of buying, selling, merging, or restructuring a business. These transactions require careful handling of ownership, liabilities, compliance, and contractual obligations to avoid future disputes or legal exposure.

Common Issues Clients Face

  • Buying or selling a business without proper legal due diligence
  • Unclear valuation and transfer terms
  • Hidden liabilities discovered after acquisition
  • Disputes between exiting and continuing partners
  • Regulatory approvals and compliance delays
  • Improper transfer of assets, licenses, or contracts

How an Advocate Helps

  • Advises on transaction structure and legal feasibility
  • Conducts legal due diligence
  • Drafts and reviews transfer and acquisition agreements
  • Ensures statutory and regulatory compliance
  • Protects against future liabilities and disputes

Primary Steps Clients Should Follow

Step 1: Define the Nature of the Transaction

Identify whether the transaction is a merger, acquisition, share transfer, asset transfer, or business sale.

Step 2: Conduct Legal Due Diligence
  • Review company records and contracts
  • Check compliance and pending liabilities
  • Identify legal risks and obligations
Step 3: Draft and Finalize Agreements

Proper agreements define consideration, representations, warranties, and exit obligations.

Step 4: Complete Transfers and Filings
  • Execution of transfer deeds and agreements
  • Regulatory filings and approvals
  • Transfer of assets, licenses, and ownership

Mistakes Clients Should Avoid

  • Proceeding without legal due diligence
  • Relying only on verbal understandings
  • Ignoring hidden liabilities or compliance gaps
  • Improper documentation of transfers
  • Delaying statutory filings after the transaction

Common M&A and Business Transfer Matters
